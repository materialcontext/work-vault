# Modern China: Alternative Paths to Development
## 11-Hour 1-on-1 Online Course Outline for Global Studies

### Course Overview
**Duration**: 11 one-hour sessions  
**Format**: 1-on-1 online instruction  
**Essential Question**: *How has China's unique approach to development and governance shaped modern Chinese society, and what can we learn by comparing it to Iran's path?*

---

## Hour 1: Geographic Foundations and Identity Formation
**Opening Hook**: "If Iran is about 1/6 the size of the United States, China is roughly the same size as the US. How might this shape their different development paths?"

### Session Structure:
- **Opening Discussion (10 min)**: Student's existing knowledge and perceptions of China
- **Interactive Map Exploration (15 min)**: 
  - Compare China's geography to Iran's
  - Identify major regions, rivers, and geographic challenges
  - Discuss how geography shapes identity (building on Unit 1)
- **Primary Source Analysis (20 min)**: 
  - Lu Xun's "My Old Home" (excerpt) - change and nostalgia
  - Connect to Iranian identity struggles with modernization
- **Comparative Framework Introduction (10 min)**:
  - How do we compare two different revolutionary paths?
  - Preview: Economic first vs. ideology first approaches
- **Closing Reflection (5 min)**: One similarity and one difference already noticed between China and Iran

**Reading for Next Session**: *Wild Swans* excerpt (3 generations perspective)

---

## Hour 2: Imperial Collapse to Republican Era (1900-1949)
**Opening Question**: "Both Iran and China had ancient empires. Why did China's completely collapse while Iran's transformed?"

### Session Structure:
- **Review and Connect (10 min)**: Discuss *Wild Swans* reading
- **Historical Overview Discussion (20 min)**:
  - Last Emperor to Warlord Era
  - May Fourth Movement - youth demanding change (connect to student activism today)
  - Compare to Iran's Constitutional Revolution (1905-1911)
- **Source Analysis (15 min)**: 
  - Chen Duxiu's "Our Final Awakening" (1916) - youth voice
  - Discuss: How do young people drive historical change?
- **Comparative Timeline Activity (10 min)**:
  - Student creates parallel timeline: China and Iran 1900-1950
- **Discussion (5 min)**: Why did foreign pressure affect these countries differently?

**Optional Reading**: Selections from *Red Star Over China*

---

## Hour 3: The Communist Revolution - A Different Kind of Revolution
**Opening Paradox**: "How did a peasant revolution in the world's most populous country succeed where urban revolutions failed?"

### Session Structure:
- **Opening Discussion (10 min)**: What makes a revolution successful? (Building on Iran unit)
- **Interactive Storytelling (20 min)**:
  - The Long March as epic journey
  - Mao's peasant focus vs. Iran's urban coalition
  - Role of ideology vs. pragmatism
- **Primary Source Comparison (15 min)**:
  - Mao's "Report on Hunan Peasants" vs. Khomeini's speeches
  - Different revolutionary languages and appeals
- **Discussion (10 min)**: Revolutionary strategies
  - Violence vs. non-violence
  - Role of foreign powers (USSR support vs. US opposition)
- **Reflection (5 min)**: What determines revolutionary success?

**Prep for Next Session**: Brief video on Great Leap Forward (provided)

---

## Hour 4: The Mao Era - Ideology in Power (1949-1976)
**Opening Provocation**: "What happens when revolutionary ideals meet governing reality?"

### Session Structure:
- **Video Debrief (10 min)**: Reactions to Great Leap Forward footage
- **Guided Discussion (20 min)**:
  - Great Leap Forward: intentions vs. consequences
  - Comparing to Iran's post-revolution economic struggles
  - The cost of ideological purity
- **Cultural Revolution Focus (20 min)**:
  - Read together: *Red Scarf Girl* excerpt
  - Discuss: How do societies turn children against parents?
  - Connect to identity formation and peer pressure
- **Comparative Analysis (10 min)**:
  - China's continuous revolution vs. Iran's consolidated theocracy
  - Different approaches to maintaining revolutionary spirit

**Homework Reflection**: Write 1 paragraph on "unintended consequences" in both revolutions

---

## Hour 5: The Turning Point - Deng's Reforms (1978-1989)
**Opening Quote**: "It doesn't matter if a cat is black or white, as long as it catches mice" - What does this mean?

### Session Structure:
- **Homework Discussion (10 min)**: Share reflections on unintended consequences
- **Economic Reform Exploration (20 min)**:
  - Special Economic Zones explained through simple analogies
  - "Socialism with Chinese characteristics" - what does this mean?
  - Compare to Iran's resistance to economic liberalization
- **Case Study Discussion (15 min)**:
  - Shenzhen: From fishing village to megacity
  - What enabled this transformation?
- **Democracy Movement Context (10 min)**:
  - Wei Jingsheng's "Fifth Modernization"
  - Seeds of Tiananmen
- **Closing Reflection (5 min)**: Can economic freedom exist without political freedom?

**Reading**: Excerpts from *Age of Ambition* by Evan Osnos

---

## Hour 6: Tiananmen and Its Aftermath - A Conversation
**Note**: This session requires special sensitivity

### Session Structure:
- **Opening Context (15 min)**: 
  - What we know from multiple sources
  - Why this matters for understanding modern China
  - Establishing discussion norms
- **Document Analysis (20 min)**:
  - Student perspectives from 1989
  - Government perspective
  - International responses
  - Compare to Iran's suppression of protests
- **Thoughtful Discussion (20 min)**:
  - How do governments and citizens remember events differently?
  - The role of historical memory in shaping society
  - Connection to current global protests
- **Closing Reflection (5 min)**: Processing complex emotions about historical events

**Optional Reading**: Personal accounts from various perspectives

---

## Hour 7: China's Economic Miracle - The Factory of the World
**Opening Activity**: "Look around your room - how many things say 'Made in China'?"

### Session Structure:
- **Personal Connection (10 min)**: Mapping student's daily life to Chinese production
- **Economic Data Exploration (20 min)**:
  - Interactive graphs: China's GDP growth vs. Iran's
  - 800 million lifted from poverty - how?
  - Environmental and social costs
- **Reading Discussion (15 min)**: 
  - *Factory Girls* excerpt - personal stories of migration
  - Compare to Iran's rural-urban migration
- **Global Impact Discussion (10 min)**:
  - How China changed global economics
  - Winners and losers from China's rise
- **Reflection (5 min)**: Is rapid development worth the costs?

**Prep**: Watch "American Factory" clip (provided)

---

## Hour 8: Modern Chinese Society - Between Tradition and Change
**Opening Question**: "How do you maintain 5,000 years of culture while becoming a modern society?"

### Session Structure:
- **Documentary Discussion (10 min)**: Reactions to "American Factory" clip
- **Identity Exploration (20 min)**:
  - One-child policy and its effects (compare to Iran's family planning)
  - Generational differences in values
  - Urban vs. rural divide
- **Cultural Expression (15 min)**:
  - Modern Chinese art, music, and literature
  - How culture navigates political restrictions
  - Compare to post-revolution Iranian culture
- **Youth Perspectives (10 min)**:
  - "Lying flat" movement and youth pressure
  - Compare to American youth concerns
- **Discussion (5 min)**: What defines Chinese identity today?

**Reading**: Contemporary Chinese short story (choice of 3)

---

## Hour 9: China's Political System - Understanding One-Party Rule
**Opening Framework**: "Different systems, similar goals - how do governments serve their people?"

### Session Structure:
- **Story Discussion (10 min)**: Insights from chosen short story
- **System Analysis (25 min)**:
  - How does China's government actually work?
  - Meritocracy vs. democracy debates
  - Compare to Iran's hybrid system
  - Local experiments in governance
- **Case Studies (15 min)**:
  - How China handled COVID (compare to other systems)
  - Poverty alleviation campaigns
  - Technology governance
- **Critical Discussion (10 min)**: Trade-offs between stability and freedom
  - Different cultural values about governance
  - Avoiding ethnocentric judgments

**Prep**: Current event article about China (student choice)

---

## Hour 10: China in the World - From Isolation to Integration
**Opening Map**: "The New Silk Road - what does China want?"

### Session Structure:
- **Current Event Discussion (10 min)**: Student's chosen article
- **Global Influence Exploration (20 min)**:
  - Belt and Road Initiative explained
  - China in Africa, Latin America, and Asia
  - Compare to Iran's regional influence strategy
- **Technology and Power (15 min)**:
  - China's tech companies going global
  - Digital sovereignty debates
  - Different internet ecosystems
- **US-China Relations (10 min)**:
  - Competition and cooperation
  - How history shapes current tensions
- **Reflection (5 min)**: What kind of global power is China becoming?

**Final Prep**: Think about our essential question

---

## Hour 11: Synthesis - Lessons from Two Paths
**Final Essential Question**: "What can we learn from comparing China and Iran's different responses to modernization?"

### Session Structure:
- **Opening Reflection (10 min)**: 
  - Student's evolved understanding of China
  - Most surprising discoveries
- **Comparative Analysis Discussion (20 min)**:
  - Revolutionary goals vs. outcomes in both countries
  - Economic development vs. ideological purity
  - Managing tradition and modernity
  - International integration vs. isolation
- **Contemporary Connections (15 min)**:
  - What these histories teach us about current events
  - Lessons for other developing nations
  - Personal relevance to student's life
- **Final Project Planning (10 min)**:
  - Student chooses final reflection format:
    - Comparative essay (5 paragraphs)
    - Recorded presentation (10 minutes)
    - Creative piece with explanation
    - Letter exchange between Chinese and Iranian students
- **Course Closure (5 min)**: 
  - Key takeaways
  - Remaining questions
  - Resources for continued learning

## Course Design Features for 1-on-1 Online Format:

### **Discussion-Centered Learning**
- Each session built around conversation rather than lecture
- Socratic questioning to develop critical thinking
- Personal connections to maintain engagement

### **Flexible Pacing**
- Built-in adjustment time in each session
- Can spend more time on student interests
- Optional readings for engaged students

### **Comparative Framework**
- Constant connection to Iran unit
- Develops analytical skills through comparison
- Avoids simplistic judgments

### **Low-Stakes Engagement**
- Reflections rather than formal assessments
- Choice in readings and final project
- Focus on understanding over memorization

### **Sensitive Topic Handling**
- Advance notice for difficult topics
- Multiple perspectives presented
- Space for processing and questions

### **Resources Required**
- Video conferencing platform with screen share
- Access to provided readings (PDFs)
- Interactive maps and timeline tools
- Selected video clips (provided as links)

This structure maximizes the advantages of 1-on-1 instruction while building meaningfully on previous units and preparing students for sophisticated global thinking.
