🚶‍♂️ How People Came to America

🌉 The Long-Held Explanation of How

As many as eight to one hundred million indigenous people lived in the Americas by the time Christopher Columbus arrived. They occupied lands throughout the North and South American continents ranging from the Arctic—now Alaska and northern Canada—south to the tip of South America, and all points in between.

🧊 The Land Bridge Theory

  • During Ice Age, sea levels were much lower
  • Land bridge connected Asia and North America
  • People followed animal herds across
  • This happened around 15,000-20,000 years ago

🌊 Alternative Theories

  • Some may have come by boat along the coast
  • Multiple waves of migration over time
  • Archaeological evidence still being discovered
  • Native oral traditions tell different stories

🔍 What the Evidence Shows

Archaeologists have found tools, bones, and other artifacts that help piece together this ancient migration. But new discoveries keep changing our understanding!

🏛️ Indigenous People of Central and South America

Three of the most highly established and recognized indigenous groups of the Americas were the Maya, Aztec, and the Inca.

🏗️ Maya

  • Mexico, Honduras, and northern Guatemala
  • Sophisticated calendar system
  • Advanced mathematics (concept of zero)
  • Impressive pyramids, which they used for astronomy
  • Only fully developed writing system in pre-Columbian America

⚔️ Aztec

  • Central Mexico region
  • Powerful military empire
  • Built Tenochtitlan on a lake
  • Advanced engineering and agriculture
  • Complex religious and social systems

🏔️ Inca

  • Andes Mountains of South America
  • Massive road network across mountains
  • Incredible stone architecture (Machu Picchu)
  • Quipu system for record-keeping
  • Terraced farming in steep terrain

🏜️ The American Southwest

📍 Geographic Focus

The American Southwest includes what is now Arizona, New Mexico, Colorado, Utah, and parts of Texas and California.

🏺 Ancestral Puebloans (Anasazi)

  • Lived from about 100 to 1300 CE
  • Built amazing cliff dwellings
  • Mesa Verde and Canyon de Chelly
  • Advanced pottery and basket weaving
  • Mysteriously abandoned their cities around 1300

🌽 Pueblo Peoples

  • Descendants of Ancestral Puebloans
  • Hopi, Zuni, and Rio Grande Pueblos
  • Adobe buildings and community plazas
  • Sophisticated farming in desert environment
  • Still living in the Southwest today

🏗️ Architectural Marvels

The cliff dwellings at Mesa Verde show incredible engineering skills - built into steep canyon walls with rooms that stayed cool in summer and warm in winter!

⛵ The Earliest Europeans in America

🧭 Before Columbus

Quite possibly, the first Europeans to make the Atlantic journey across to North America were not Christopher Columbus and the Spanish, but the Norwegian sailors and explorers.

⚔️ Vikings (Norse)

  • Led by Erik the Red and Leif Erikson
  • Reached North America around 1000 CE
  • Established settlements in Greenland
  • Evidence of settlements in Newfoundland
  • 500 years before Columbus!

🌍 Why Vikings Were First

  • Excellent shipbuilding and navigation
  • Used "island hopping" route via Iceland
  • Shorter ocean distances than southern routes
  • Established base in Greenland first
  • Left archaeological evidence at L'Anse aux Meadows
