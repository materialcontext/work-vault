The Neutral Starting Point

America began WWI with balanced trade relationships and minimal financial entanglements - true neutrality was still economically possible.

🚢 Stage 1: The Trade Boom Decision (1914-1915)

Choosing Profit Over Strict Neutrality
ECONOMIC OPPORTUNITY:
European war creates massive demand for American goods - food, raw materials, manufactured products
CHOICE:
Strict neutrality (embargo all belligerents) vs. Selective trade vs. Open commerce with all buyers
DECISION:
Allow unrestricted trade with all belligerents while maintaining "legal" neutrality
CONSEQUENCE:
British blockade eliminates German trade; American prosperity becomes tied to Allied purchases
NEW SITUATION:
American farmers, manufacturers, and workers dependent on continued Allied demand

🏦 Stage 2: The Lending Decision (1915-1916)

From Trade to Financial Stakeholder
ECONOMIC OPPORTUNITY:
Allies need massive loans to continue purchasing American goods and fighting the war
CHOICE:
Prohibit all loans to belligerents vs. Allow private lending vs. Government lending
DECISION:
Reverse Bryan's loan ban - allow private banks to finance Allied purchases
CONSEQUENCE:
J.P. Morgan organizes $500M loan (largest in Wall Street history) - American banks now stakeholders in Allied victory
NEW SITUATION:
Allied defeat would trigger American financial crisis - neutrality becomes economically dangerous

🕸️ Stage 3: The Morgan Web Expands (1916-1917)

Creating Systemic Financial Dependency
ECONOMIC OPPORTUNITY:
Allies need even more financing as war costs escalate beyond initial projections
CHOICE:
Limit further lending vs. Gradual expansion vs. Unlimited financial support
DECISION:
Allow J.P. Morgan to organize $2.5B in Allied loans vs. only $27M to Central Powers
CONSEQUENCE:
American financial system becomes dependent on Allied success - Morgan earns $30M in commissions from $3B in purchases
NEW SITUATION:
Allied defeat means collapse of American banking system and economic depression

⚠️ Stage 4: Economic Point of No Return (1917)

When Neutrality Becomes Economic Suicide
ECONOMIC CRISIS:
German submarine warfare threatens to cut off Allied ability to pay back loans and continue purchases
CHOICE:
Accept economic collapse vs. Find face-saving exit vs. Ensure Allied victory through war
DECISION:
War declaration to protect American financial stability by ensuring Allied victory
CONSEQUENCE:
American entry tips balance toward Allied victory, protecting $2.5B in loans and trade relationships
FINAL RESULT:
America emerges as global financial power, but critics argue war fought for Wall Street profits
