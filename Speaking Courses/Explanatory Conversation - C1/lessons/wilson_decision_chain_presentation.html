📚 Wilson Context

🇺🇸 Wilson's Path to War

The Decision-Consequence Chain That Led America to WWI

How events created choices, choices created consequences, and consequences created new events in an escalating cycle toward war

"The world must be made safe for democracy" - Woodrow Wilson, April 2, 1917

🧱 Our Language Frame

"When [event] happened, America faced a choice between... The decision to [choice] led to [consequence], which then created a new situation where..."

The Power of This Frame

This structure shows how events created decision points, and decisions created new events, in an escalating cycle toward war.

⚖️ Wilson's Political Tightrope

🗳️ Electoral Weakness

  • Won only 42% of vote in 1912
  • Razor-thin 1916 victory (3,773 votes in California)
  • Campaigned on "He Kept Us Out of War"
  • No strong popular mandate

🌍 Coalition Pressures

  • German-Americans (8-10% of population)
  • Irish-Americans hostile to helping Britain
  • Economic interests tied to Allied victory
  • Regional divisions (Midwest vs. Northeast)
"Wilson lacked a strong mandate and faced complex constituencies with conflicting views on the European war"

🚢 Stage 1: The Lusitania Crisis (1915)

The Decision-Consequence Chain Begins
EVENT:
German U-boat sinks Lusitania on May 7, 1915, killing 1,195 people including 128 Americans
CHOICE:
Diplomatic protest vs. Military retaliation vs. Economic pressure
DECISION:
Strong diplomatic protest with implicit threat of future action
CONSEQUENCE:
Germany temporarily restricts submarine warfare
NEW SITUATION:
Appearance of American influence, but precedent of threats without follow-through

⚓ Stage 2: The Sussex Incident (1916)

Escalating the Pressure
EVENT:
More ships sunk despite German promises - Sussex attacked March 1916
CHOICE:
Accept violations vs. Ultimatum vs. Break diplomatic relations
DECISION:
Sussex Pledge ultimatum - "stop unrestricted warfare or we break relations"
CONSEQUENCE:
Germany agrees to warn before attacking and ensure passenger safety
NEW SITUATION:
America committed to escalation if Germany resumes unrestricted warfare

🌊 Stage 3: Return of Unrestricted Submarine Warfare (1917)

The Trap Springs
EVENT:
Germany announces resumption of unrestricted submarine warfare February 1, 1917
CHOICE:
Accept humiliation vs. Armed neutrality vs. Declare war immediately
DECISION:
Armed neutrality - arm merchant ships for self-defense
CONSEQUENCE:
Direct confrontation becomes inevitable as armed ships engage U-boats
NEW SITUATION:
America already in undeclared naval war with Germany

📨 Stage 4: The Zimmermann Telegram (1917)

The Final Straw
EVENT:
Intercepted German proposal for Mexican alliance to recover Texas, New Mexico, Arizona
CHOICE:
Keep secret vs. Limited response vs. Full war declaration
DECISION:
War declaration - "The world must be made safe for democracy"
CONSEQUENCE:
America enters European war with 373-50 House vote, 82-6 Senate vote
FINAL RESULT:
Reluctant superpower born from escalating decisions, not single choice

Key Insight

Each decision created new constraints that made the next decision more difficult and war more likely. Wilson didn't choose war in April 1917 - he chose it gradually through three years of escalating commitments.
